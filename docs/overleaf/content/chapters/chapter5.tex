\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{ch:evaluation}

This chapter details the experimental evaluation of SemanticMesh. We describe the datasets used for evaluation, explain the evaluation metrics and methodology (including both standard RAGAS metrics and a custom AI-as-a-Judge framework), outline the design of the 21 ablation experiments, and analyze the empirical results to quantify the impact of each architectural component.

\section{Experimental Setup and Datasets}
\label{sec:eval:datasets}

The framework is evaluated across a suite of synthetic datasets designed to simulate varying levels of schema complexity and documentation styles. The test suite includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{E-Commerce Baseline (DS01)}: A standard retail schema containing 7 tables and 15 question-answer pairs, accompanied by a clean business glossary.
    \item \textbf{Stress Test (DS07)}: A large-scale schema designed to test the limits of context windows, parallel processing, and entity resolution blocking.
\end{itemize}
Each dataset includes a DDL file, business documentation, and a human-curated ``Gold Standard'' mapping and Q\&A evaluation bundle.

\section{Evaluation Methodology and Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:metrics}

To capture the diverse failure modes of RAG and schema mapping, the evaluation framework implements two distinct metric groups.

\subsection{Standard RAGAS Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:ragas}

The system integrates four core metrics from the RAGAS framework~\cite{es2025ragasautomatedevaluationretrieval} (including the quality aspects proposed in the original paper alongside library-specific extensions):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Faithfulness}: Measures the extent to which the generated answer is grounded in the retrieved context, capturing hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{Answer Relevancy}: Evaluates how well the generated answer addresses the user query, penalizing incomplete or off-topic responses.
    \item \textbf{Context Precision} (extending the paper's original \emph{Context Relevance}): Measures the signal-to-noise ratio of retrieved chunks, validating the reranker's ability to rank relevant passages first.
    \item \textbf{Context Recall}: Evaluates whether the retrieval engine captured all relevant facts needed to answer the question, validating retrieval coverage against human annotations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Custom Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:custom_metrics}

Two custom metrics evaluate pipeline-specific self-reflection and alignment performance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cypher Healing Rate}: Computed as the ratio of successfully healed Cypher queries to the total number of queries that failed initial dry-run syntax verification.
    \item \textbf{HITL Confidence Agreement}: Measures the correlation between the mapping confidence score computed by the Actor-Critic validator and the actual semantic correctness of the alignment against the gold standard.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Custom AI Judge Framework}
\label{sec:eval:ai_judge}

Standard automated metrics like RAGAS often penalize technical responses containing database identifiers or structured schemas. We therefore implement a custom LLM-as-a-Judge framework powered by \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano-2026-03-17}. The AI Judge evaluates generated answers across five weighted dimensions using a structured rubric:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Builder Quality} ($25\%$): Evaluates the correctness of triplet extraction, entity resolution deduplication, and schema mapping validation.
    \item \textbf{Retrieval Effectiveness} ($25\%$): Evaluates whether the hybrid retrieval (vector, BM25, and graph) correctly captured the required context.
    \item \textbf{Answer Quality} ($30\%$): Evaluates the factual accuracy, completeness, and clarity of the generated answer.
    \item \textbf{Pipeline Health} ($10\%$): Evaluates the execution health of self-reflection loops (Cypher healing, mapping validation, and grading).
    \item \textbf{Ablation Impact} ($10\%$): Evaluates the qualitative impact of the enabled/disabled pipeline components under the active study configuration.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ablation Study Design}
\label{sec:eval:ablation_design}

To measure the contribution of individual components, we conduct an ablation campaign comprising 21 distinct experiments (AB-00 through AB-20) plus an optimized configuration (AB-BEST):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{AB-00 (Baseline)}: Default settings with all modules enabled.
    \item \textbf{AB-01 \& AB-02}: Retrieval mode ablations (vector-only and BM25-only).
    \item \textbf{AB-03 to AB-05}: Reranker ablations (reranker disabled, top\_k=5, top\_k=20).
    \item \textbf{AB-06 to AB-08}: Chunking strategy modifications (128/16, 384/48, 512/64).
    \item \textbf{AB-09 \& AB-10}: Triplet extraction token limit variations (4096 and 16384 tokens).
    \item \textbf{AB-11 \& AB-12}: Entity resolution similarity threshold (0.65 and 0.85).
    \item \textbf{AB-13 \& AB-14}: Entity resolution blocking K-NN search size variations (top\_k=5 and top\_k=20).
    \item \textbf{AB-15 \& AB-16}: Component-off studies, disabling schema enrichment and Actor-Critic validation respectively.
    \item \textbf{AB-17 \& AB-18}: Human-in-the-loop (HITL) confidence threshold variations (0.70 and 0.85).
    \item \textbf{AB-19 \& AB-20}: Component-off studies, disabling Cypher healing and hallucination grading respectively.
    \item \textbf{AB-BEST}: Optimised configuration combining the best parameters identified during the ablation runs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Retrieval Mode Impact}
\label{sec:eval:retrieval_impact}

The results confirm hybrid retrieval as the robust default. On this simple dataset the three retrieval modes are barely separated by the judge (hybrid $4.50$, vector-only $4.25$, BM25-only $4.25$), but the retrieval signals still point the same way as on harder datasets. Disabling vector search (AB-02, BM25-only) collapses Gold Standard (GT) coverage to $54\%$, since keyword search cannot match semantically paraphrased terms. Vector-only (AB-01) keeps full GT coverage ($100\%$) but its retrieval-quality score drops markedly, because the cross-encoder is fed dense-only candidates. Hybrid retrieval (dense + keyword + graph traversal) is the only mode that preserves both GT coverage and a high retrieval score, and is therefore retained in AB-BEST.

\subsection{Reranker and Context Window Optimization}
\label{sec:eval:reranker_impact}

On DS01 the reranker setting does not discriminate quality: restricting the output to the top 5 chunks (AB-04) and expanding it to the top 20 (AB-05) both score $4.50/5.0$ with full GT coverage ($100\%$), and even disabling the reranker entirely (AB-03) lands at $4.65/5.0$. The dataset is small enough that any of the three retrieves the expected sources. The wider context window ($\texttt{top\_k=20}$) was validated across all seven datasets (Section~\ref{sec:eval:topk_sensitivity}): there the two settings are again effectively tied at the judge level ($4.31$ vs $4.28$ average), so AB-BEST retains \texttt{top\_k=5} for the $4\times$ cross-encoder inference saving without sacrificing qualitative performance.

\subsection{Pipeline Validation and Enrichment}
\label{sec:eval:validation_impact}

On the v1.5.1 DS01 baseline, disabling schema-mapping validation and enrichment does \emph{not} degrade retrieval coverage: both Actor-Critic validation OFF (AB-16) and schema enrichment OFF (AB-15) retain $98\%$ GT coverage and judge scores within noise of the baseline ($4.40$ and $4.50$). The earlier GT-coverage collapse observed for these components (to $67\%$ and $61\%$ on pre-v1.5.1 runs) does not reproduce on this simple 7-table schema, where the downstream graph still retrieves the expected sources even without acronym expansion or critic validation. Their value is expected to resurface on larger or degraded schemas (DS05, DS07); they are therefore kept ON in AB-BEST for robustness. The one pipeline component whose absence the judge still penalises on DS01 is Cypher healing (AB-19, $3.80/5.0$): even when all tables complete, unhealed Cypher leaves the graph structurally weaker.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Generalisation and Top-K Sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval:topk_sensitivity}

The DS01 ablation isolates single variables on a simple baseline; the value of the optimised configuration (AB-BEST) appears on the harder DS02--DS07, where it sustains $100\%$ grounded answers across all $210$ questions (zero hallucinations) and $100\%$ builder completion, including the $58$-table stress set. Its average AI-Judge score over the seven datasets is $4.31/5.0$ under the systematic \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} judge, with the hardest cases being DS03 Healthcare ($3.65$, multi-hop clinical questions) and the degraded schemas DS06 legacy ($4.25$) and DS07 stress ($4.20$).

To validate the reranker pool size beyond DS01, the full pipeline was re-run with \texttt{reranker\_top\_k=20} (AB-BEST-K20) on all seven datasets. The two configurations are effectively tied at the judge level: AB-BEST (K5) averages $4.31$ versus AB-BEST-K20 $4.28$ ($\Delta = -0.03$); K5 wins three datasets, K20 wins two, and two are tied. K20 retrieves strictly more expected sources on every dataset (average GT coverage $0.986$ vs $0.860$, largest gains on the degraded schemas DS06 $+0.37$ and DS05 $+0.21$), but that recall advantage does not translate into higher answer quality --- on complex schemas the extra chunks at ranks 6--20 are often related-but-wrong tables that dilute precision. AB-BEST therefore retains \texttt{top\_k=5} on efficiency grounds (a $4\times$ reduction in cross-encoder inference) rather than quality dominance.

\subsection{Retrieval Quality Score Floor and Dual Metrics}
\label{sec:eval:dual_metrics}

A measurement caveat surfaced during the v1.5.1 campaign: cross-encoder (CE) absolute scores are artificially low on highly technical, multi-section content such as DDL and data dictionaries. A chunk that is unambiguously the best match for a query may receive a raw CE score of $\sim 0.15$, which would cause the retrieval-quality gate to flag it as poor and distort downstream confidence heuristics --- a ``floor issue''.

To address this without weakening the gate, the rerank node exports two metrics: \texttt{retrieval\_quality\_score\_raw} (the unmodified CE score of the top chunk) and \texttt{retrieval\_quality\_score\_adjusted} (a pool-aware floor). When the candidate pool exceeds a minimum size (\texttt{pool\_confidence\_min\_size}) and the raw score is below a ceiling (\texttt{pool\_confidence\_ceiling}), the score is floored to a minimum acceptable value (\texttt{pool\_confidence\_floor}). The rationale is that being ranked first in a large, competitive pool is stronger evidence of relevance than the raw absolute CE score implies. The quality gate consumes the adjusted score (avoiding false rejections on technical documents) while the raw score is preserved in the evaluation bundle for analysis. The \texttt{pool\_confidence\_applied} flag records when the adjustment was triggered.
