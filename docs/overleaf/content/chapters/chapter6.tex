\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This chapter concludes the thesis. It first restates the primary contributions of SemanticMesh against the empirical evidence gathered in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}, then discusses the system's limitations---distinguishing limitations that were actually observed during the evaluation from those that remain plausible design extrapolations---and finally outlines directions for future research.

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion:contributions}

This thesis presented SemanticMesh, a multi-agent framework that automates semantic discovery and data governance by bridging the semantic gap between unstructured business documentation and physical database schemas, constructing a queryable Knowledge Graph in Neo4j. Its primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Two-graph architecture (CQRS for LLM orchestration).} A strict separation between a write-heavy \emph{Builder} graph that constructs the ontology from governance documents and a read-heavy \emph{Query} graph that performs agentic retrieval-augmented generation. The separation enables independent execution, incremental re-ingestion, and a clean isolation between graph-construction correctness and query-time latency.
    \item \textbf{Multi-stage entity resolution.} A two-stage pipeline---BGE-M3 vector blocking with Union-Find clustering at a cosine threshold, followed by a mid-tier LLM judge (\texttt{ER\_JUDGE\_SYSTEM})---that deduplicates business concepts across heterogeneous documents. The embedding-based first stage keeps the judge's workload tractable while preserving recall.
    \item \textbf{Self-reflection loops for quality assurance.} Three independent correction mechanisms: (i) Actor-Critic schema-mapping validation, in which a single mid-tier LLM plays two prompt-separated roles---Actor and Critic---in a Self-Refine-style generate-feedback-refine loop; (ii) Cypher healing, where generated queries are dry-run via \texttt{EXPLAIN} and syntax errors are fed back for correction, with a deterministic parameterised builder as a guaranteed fallback; and (iii) a Self-RAG-style hallucination grader that gates the final answer against the retrieved context.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion.} A retrieval strategy combining dense vector search (primarily over a \texttt{businessconcept\_embedding} index), BM25 full-text search, and variable-depth graph traversal, fused by Reciprocal Rank Fusion and re-ranked by a cross-encoder (\texttt{bge-reranker-v2-m3}).
    \item \textbf{Ablation-backed design.} A systematic campaign of 21 single-variable studies (AB-00--AB-20) plus the optimised AB-BEST and AB-BEST-K20 configurations, evaluated by a domain-specific, architecture-aware LLM-as-a-Judge rather than generic metrics. The ablation evidence---not a single winner-takes-all score---informs the default configuration, as detailed in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}.
    \item \textbf{Provider-agnostic LLM factory and incremental ingestion.} A five-tier LLM factory abstracting twelve provider backends behind a uniform \texttt{LLMProtocol}, and a SHA-256 file-registry that detects changed documents and triggers partial reprocessing without rebuilding the whole graph.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical Summary}
\label{sec:conclusion:empirical}

Evaluated across seven synthetic datasets (111 tables, 210 questions) under the systematic \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} judge, the AB-BEST configuration produced \textbf{210/210 grounded answers (100\%), zero hallucinations, and 100\% builder completion}, including the 58-table stress set (DS07). Its average AI-Judge score was \textbf{4.31/5}, with the hardest cases being DS03 Healthcare (3.65, multi-hop clinical questions) and the degraded schemas DS06 legacy (4.25) and DS07 stress (4.20). Two findings of the ablation campaign deserve emphasis because they revise intuitive expectations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The simple baseline does not discriminate components.} On DS01 the judge is tightly compressed (3.80--4.80), so most single-variable changes are indistinguishable from the baseline (4.50). The only component whose absence the judge penalises on this dataset is Cypher healing (AB-19, 3.80); schema enrichment and Actor-Critic validation remain within noise of the baseline and are retained for robustness on degraded or larger schemas rather than for measurable DS01 quality.
    \item \textbf{Retrieval recall and answer quality are decoupled.} Expanding the reranker pool from $k{=}5$ to $k{=}20$ raised average Gold-Standard coverage from $0.860$ to $0.986$ but did \emph{not} raise the judge score (4.31 vs.\ 4.28). On complex schemas the extra chunks at ranks 6--20 are often related-but-wrong tables that dilute precision. AB-BEST therefore keeps $k{=}5$ for a $4\times$ cross-encoder inference saving without sacrificing qualitative performance.
\end{itemize}
A measurement caveat also surfaced: cross-encoder absolute scores are artificially low on highly technical content (DDL, data dictionaries), so the rerank node exports both a raw and a pool-aware \emph{adjusted} retrieval-quality score, with the quality gate consuming the adjusted value to avoid false rejections on technical documents.

\section{System Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion:limitations}

We distinguish limitations observed during the evaluation from design limitations that remain plausible but unmeasured extrapolations.

\textbf{Observed limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-hop clinical precision.} On DS03 Healthcare the system stays fully grounded and 94\% covered, yet the judge penalises answer precision (3.65) on multi-hop clinical questions where several tables must be jointly reasoned over.
    \item \textbf{Degraded-schema coverage.} On incomplete or legacy schemas (DS05, DS06) Gold-Standard coverage drops to 79\% and 63\% respectively---the documentation gaps propagate into the constructed graph and cannot be fully recovered even with the wider $k{=}20$ window.
    \item \textbf{Retrieval-quality score floor.} As noted above, raw cross-encoder scores on technical content are artificially low and require a pool-aware adjustment; the floor is a workaround, not a fundamental fix.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Design limitations (qualitative, not yet measured at the thresholds stated).}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Wide tables.} Tables with a very large number of columns are expected to degrade schema-mapping performance as the dense schema representation consumes model context; the evaluated fixtures do not exercise this regime (the largest fixture is DS07 with 58 tables, and column counts stay well below the regime where pressure would manifest).
    \item \textbf{Scale of global reasoning.} Global queries over schemas far larger than those evaluated are expected to approach output-token limits and lose traversal precision; no evaluated dataset reaches that scale.
    \item \textbf{Embedding drift on specialised vocabulary.} A general-purpose embedder such as BGE-M3 can lose precision on highly specialised corporate vocabulary; this is a known property of the model rather than a measured degradation on the present datasets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:conclusion:future_work}

Several directions follow directly from the limitations above and from the architecture's current boundaries:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Community detection for global reasoning.} SemanticMesh currently has no graph-level community-detection stage (unlike Microsoft GraphRAG's Leiden-based hierarchical clustering~\cite{blondel2008fast, traag2019from}). Incorporating Louvain or Leiden community detection to partition large schemas into localised sub-graphs would enable hierarchical map-reduce answering of broad, global questions and is the most promising route to the multi-hop and large-scale limitations.
    \item \textbf{Semantic intra-table chunking.} Clustering the columns of wide tables into semantic subgroups before mapping would reduce context-window pressure and is expected to improve mapping precision on wide schemas.
    \item \textbf{Domain-specific embedding or SLM fine-tuning.} Fine-tuning a small language model (e.g.\ via Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning~\cite{hu2022lora}) on the corporate document corpus would address embedding drift and reduce the cost of the extraction and entity-resolution tiers.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive retrieval pool.} Because $k{=}20$ strictly improves recall without hurting judged quality, an adaptive policy that raises the pool size when the adjusted retrieval-quality score is low could capture the recall gain only where the answer is under-supported, paying the cross-encoder cost only when needed.
    \item \textbf{Stronger text-to-Cypher generation.} Although the single-statement guard and deterministic fallback make Cypher generation robust, a dedicated text-to-Cypher model~\cite{tiwari2025autocypherimprovingllmscypher} could further reduce the residual multi-statement emissions and the fallback load.
    \item \textbf{Active learning from human-in-the-loop review.} Persisting HITL mapping corrections to refine future mapping proposals and system prompts would let the system improve with use, directly targeting the precision gap on multi-hop and degraded-schema questions.
\end{itemize}
